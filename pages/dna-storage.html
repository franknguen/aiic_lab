DNA storage

Research field

DNA can store data at a density no silicon memory approaches, and keeps it for centuries — but writing and reading it (synthesis and sequencing) introduce substitutions, insertions and deletions. Error-correcting codes designed for this channel are what make DNA a storage medium rather than a curiosity.

Specific topics on ECC for DNA storage

  • Error-correcting codes (ECC) converted from classical codes for DNA storage
  • New designs of insertion/deletion codes that satisfy the GC-content and homopolymer-run constraints
  • LDPC code design for DNA storage
  • Run-length-limited and constrained coding for composite DNA

Basics of DNA storage: Wikipedia (properties, history, references)

In plain words

A file is turned into a long string over the four bases A, C, G, T, cut into short strands, synthesised, and later sequenced back. Two constraints make strands easier to synthesise and read: the share of G and C bases should stay near one half (GC content), and the same base should not repeat too many times in a row (homopolymer run). A code for DNA storage therefore has to correct errors and respect these constraints at the same time.

Comparison between DNA storage and other storage systems

DNA storage trades very high density and a retention time of centuries against slow, expensive writing (synthesis) and reading (sequencing), which is why it suits archival data rather than hot data.

LLR calculation of LDPC codes for DNA storage

System model

Encoding → synthesis → storage → sequencing → LLR computation → LDPC decoding. The LLR block is where the proposed strategies act.

Purpose

  • The statistics of DNA storage channels have not yet been fully analysed.
  • Decoding LDPC codes needs log-likelihood ratios (LLR) as the inputs of the variable nodes.
  • We propose two strategies: (S1) directly scaling the LLRs, and (S2) scaling the pairwise substitution error rates.

Performance comparison

  • Reading cost (Rread) versus the number of reads needed for successful decoding.
  • Over eight experiments, the proposed system shows a gain.
