Next-generation communications

Research field

Radio access for 5G, 6G and beyond: how many users can share one channel, how a base station with hundreds of antennas spends its power, and how a wall of tunable reflectors can reshape the channel itself.

Specific topics

  • 5G / 6G communication technology
  • Non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) and rate-splitting multiple access (RSMA)
  • Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS / IRS), including optical RIS for free-space optical links
  • OFDM, MIMO and massive MIMO
    • Basics of MIMO: Wikipedia (properties, history, references)
    • Basics of massive MIMO: Avnet — Understanding massive MIMO technology
  • Short-packet and ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC), UAV and satellite links, THz channels

In plain words

NOMA lets several users share the same time and frequency slot by giving them different power levels; the receiver peels the strong signal off first, then decodes the weak one. RSMA goes one step further: each user's message is split into a common part that everybody decodes and a private part that only its owner decodes, which handles interference more gracefully than either orthogonal access or NOMA. A RIS is a flat panel of many small elements whose phase shifts can be set by software, so a signal bouncing off it can be steered towards a user without any power amplifier on the panel.

Conventional cellular massive MIMO with four cells

A conventional cellular massive MIMO system with four cells: each base station carries a large antenna array and serves many single-antenna users on the same time–frequency resource, so inter-cell interference and pilot contamination set the limit.

Research trends of conventional massive MIMO systems

  • Most massive MIMO research has focused on improving channel quality, sum spectral efficiency (SE) or fairness between users.
  • None of these works demonstrates an efficient way to spend the power resource.

Recent results of the lab for massive MIMO systems

  • Based on the general definition of system energy efficiency (EE), we propose a formulation representing the EE of a cellular massive MIMO system.
  • This EE formulation is then used to form a maximisation problem with constraints on the minimum rate of every user in the system and on the power used for pilot and data signals.
  • The minimum user rate guarantees a certain quality for every user along with efficient power consumption.
  • Two iterative algorithms are proposed to solve the EE maximisation problem.
